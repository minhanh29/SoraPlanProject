System overview · Google AI builder hackathon

From floor-plan sketch to a home you can walk through

Upload a sketch and SoraPlan builds a real-scale 3D home. You can orbit it, walk through it, and move or resize furniture in centimetres. You can use our preset furniture or photos of your own pieces, which AI turns into 3D models.

Why it wins

AI room images look right but don't have to fit. Our scenes have to.

Typical AI interior tools
  • Generate a 2D image with invented proportions
  • No way to tell if the bed fits next to the wardrobe
  • One fixed camera angle
  • To change anything, you re-prompt and hope
SoraPlan
  • Every object lives in a scene measured in metres. A 2.1 m sofa is 2.1 m.
  • Collision, walkway and door-swing checks with live dimension lines
  • Walk mode, orbit and top-down views from any angle
  • Drag-and-resize editing, plus a voice architect that changes the scene for you
User flow

Three steps, one scene

Users can go back to any step at any time. All three edit the same scene file, so nothing gets out of sync.

STEP 1

Plan

Upload a sketch, PDF or screenshot. Gemini reads the walls, doors, windows, rooms and dimension labels.

  • Scale is set from the dimension labels, with door widths as a fallback
  • The user corrects the result in a 2D editor laid over the sketch
  • If the scale is uncertain, the user marks one known length
STEP 2

Style & preview

Pick a style or describe one. A designer agent picks materials and furniture, from presets or the user's uploads, and places them.

  • A plain grey render of the real layout is turned into a photoreal image by Nano Banana Pro
  • Adjust and regenerate, then approve
  • Furniture photos turn into 3D in the background
STEP 3 main selling point

3D world

Walls are built by code from the plan, and furniture models are scaled to real size.

  • Orbit with cut-away walls, top-down view and first-person walk
  • Drag, rotate and resize with snapping and live measurements
  • Warnings for collisions, walkway space and blocked doors
  • “Ask the architect” by voice, and a photo mode for high-quality stills
Core design principle

The scene graph is the single source of truth

AI suggests changes. Our own code checks and applies them. The 2D plan, the image preview and the 3D world are all drawn from the same JSON file, measured in metres. That is why proportions can't drift.

Metres everywhereData and APIs store metres. Convert to cm or ft-in only in the UI.
Never trust raw AI outputEvery AI result is checked against a schema, then for valid geometry, before it is saved.
Architecture is built by codeWalls, floors and openings are generated from the plan. Image-to-3D models never make them.
Furniture is normalisedEach model is set with its base at the floor, its front facing +Z, and its size scaled to real dimensions.
High-level architecture

Stateless services on Cloud Run, with all slow work in queued jobs

The browser builds the 3D world itself from the scene graph and CDN files, so the main feature costs almost nothing to serve. Slow AI calls run as background jobs on Cloud Tasks, and the browser watches their progress live through Firestore.

Decision record

Meshy first, Tripo as fallback, img2threejs only at dev time

Good-looking 3D objects are a hard requirement, so quality decided the main provider. img2threejs turned out to be a plugin for AI coding tools that writes Three.js code from an image. It isn't a viewer for 3D models.

Resizing tip: a stretched sofa model looks wrong. Organic furniture resizes uniformly and comes in 2–3 size variants. Boxy items such as cabinets, shelves and tables are generated from code, so they resize without distortion.

Pitch

3-minute demo script

  1. 0:00“AI room images look great, but would that sofa actually fit?” Show an AI image with impossible proportions.
  2. 0:20Upload a photo of a hand-drawn sketch. The parsed plan appears over it: “Scale from 3 dimension labels ✓”.
  3. 0:45Pick Japandi and upload a photo of my own armchair. Its pipeline runs in the background.
  4. 1:05Furnish. Rooms fill in from above, and the photoreal living-room preview matches the layout.
  5. 1:25Enter the 3D world. Orbit with cut-away walls, then walk through the door into the living room.
  6. 1:50Drag the sofa: “⚠ walkway 72 cm < 90 cm”. Resize the bed to 180 cm: “blocks wardrobe door”. Image tools can't do this.
  7. 2:15The armchair arrives as a real-size 3D model. Drag it next to the window.
  8. 2:30Say: “Swap the floor to light oak and move the TV unit 40 centimetres left.” It happens live.
  9. 2:45Photo mode, then a share link. Close on the Google stack slide.
